Practice case · Al Noor Family Clinic

Midterm practice · 50 minutes in the real exam · Use the sentence frames and name a model in every answer.

Imagined scenario: the clinic, people and numbers are invented for practice.

Al Noor Family Clinic in Doha has three doctors, four receptionists and a small lab. Six months ago it launched online booking. Since then its Google rating has fallen from 4.5 to 3.8. Reviews mention double bookings and “lost” test results. The owner wants to spend money on a new waiting room.

Two of the four receptionists started last month. They were not trained on the booking system. The new supervisor, Khalid, says “I trust everyone” and lets every receptionist decide everything alone.

When the lab finishes a test, it emails the result to reception. Reception thinks the doctor will call the patient. The doctors think reception will call. Some patients wait two weeks and then call the clinic themselves.

Task 8 above was question 1 (the SERVQUAL gaps). Now answer questions 2–4 in full sentences. In the exam, questions 2 and 3 are worth 8 marks each and question 4 is worth 16 marks.

“Khalid uses a ___ style for ___. The new receptionists need ___ because ___.”
“The gap is between ___ and ___. Nobody is accountable for ___. I would make ___ Accountable and add ___.”
“I recommend ___ first, because ___ shows ___. Compared with ___, this ___. The risk is ___, so ___. However, ___.”
Show the model answer (after you write)

2. Leadership. Khalid uses a delegating style for everyone. This suits the two experienced receptionists if they are skilled and confident. The two new receptionists are untrained on the booking system, so they need a directing style: clear steps and close checks. Untrained staff deciding alone is a likely cause of double bookings. He should direct the new staff now and move to coaching, then delegating, as they grow. However, readiness is about one task: an experienced receptionist may still need directing on the new booking system.

3. RACI. The gap is between the lab, reception and the doctors: nobody is accountable for telling the patient the result. Fix: the lab is R and A for sending the result; the doctor is R and A for reviewing it; reception is R for contacting the patient, with Khalid as A. Add a time limit, for example “patient contacted within 2 working days”, so the chart is supported by an internal SLA.

4. Recommendation. First, train the two new receptionists on the booking system, with Khalid directing them, and agree the RACI for results. The Service-Profit Chain shows the weakest link is support for staff: training fell from 6 to 1 hour a month, and the errors followed. The Balanced Scorecard shows the warning came first in learning and growth, then in customer KPIs (CSAT 84% → 72%, no-shows 6% → 11%). Revenue has not fallen yet, so acting now protects future revenue. Compared with the waiting room, this targets the biggest gap (Reliability −1.5) instead of Tangibles, which already beats expectations (+0.7). Risk: training takes receptionists off the desk; reduce it by training one person at a time in quiet hours. However, other things may also explain the falling rating, so check reviews again after one month.

Pass: the right model, applied correctly. Merit: plus numbers and facts from the case to explain causes. Distinction: plus options compared, a justified recommendation, a risk with a way to reduce it, and a limit of the model.
